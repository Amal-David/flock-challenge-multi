# Campaign lessons

- A candidate-level no-go is not a campaign-level stopping condition. After recording a refutation, immediately refresh the frontier and advance to the next non-tombstoned mechanism while the overarching promotion goal remains active.
- Preserve strict correctness and causal submission gates, but keep research, external validation, and candidate preparation continuous until a terminal promotion or an explicit user stop.
- A terminal promotion ends one objective but does not end the optimization campaign when the user explicitly asks to continue. Freeze the new frontier as the next exact base, stale prior-epoch candidates, and immediately rebase the highest-value prepared mechanism.
- Campaign greed means maximizing expected leaderboard gain per hour, not maximizing submission count. Keep at least three independent lanes alive when evidence permits: one on external validation, one materially implemented behind it, and one structural mechanism under council review. Never let an official validator or a candidate no-go make the research pipeline idle.
- Use ambitious mechanism selection—large repeated-work deletion, structural fusion, or representation changes—but require every official submission to clear the executable frontier-dominance guard. Greed expands search breadth and parallelism; it never weakens exactness, attribution, or crown-dominance evidence.
- Do not bind campaign execution to the three-hour automation cadence. The greedy optimization campaign belongs to one continuously active goal; the three-hour heartbeat is retrospective governance only and must never be interpreted as turning work on or off.
- Cloud-provider authorization is provider-specific. The user has now explicitly authorized AWS for this campaign. Reuse the retained stopped `flock-bcanonical-t0` c7i instance, encrypted volume, dedicated key, and security group before considering any new resource. Keep cloud receipts provider-specific and stop rather than terminate reusable AWS validation infrastructure unless the user says otherwise.
- A two-to-three-hour interval without a valid submission triggers a campaign retrospective and realignment, not a weaker submission gate. Verify whether the crown still leads, audit time-to-falsification and executable throughput, then change the search portfolio if councils are mostly rediscovering duplicates.
- Separate the architectural idea catalog from the exact-crown executable residual map. Before any council admits implementation, prove the mechanism is not already shipped on the live source and price net uops, traffic, spills, dependency depth, and frequency effects.
- Maintain continuous leaderboard intelligence independently of the three-hour retrospective: refresh Yukon and public validation PRs every 30 minutes, ingest terminal results and active high-EV mechanisms, and never duplicate an active public candidate.
- Production propagation follows a leaf-first sequence: source-counted exposure, standalone leaf assembly/oracle, component timing when possible, then broad integration. A patch over 200 lines cannot be the first executable probe.
- Every 90 minutes of active optimization must yield a measured receipt, a hard source tombstone, or a materially runnable candidate. Council prose and hypothesis count alone do not satisfy campaign progress.
